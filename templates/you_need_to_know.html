What You Need to Know

Before you start.

MoonBite is designed to give individuals real financial sovereignty. That power comes with real responsibility. This page tells you what that means in plain language — no hype, no sugar-coating.

Learn the basics Getting started
MoonBite is pre-mainnet, open-source software. It is not an investment or a security. Coins mined today carry no market value. There is no exchange listing yet. Nothing on this site is financial or legal advice.
Status

Where MoonBite stands today

🚧
Pre-mainnet, not production
MoonBite's testnet and regtest chains are live and fully functional. Mainnet is not yet launched. The software is real, but the network is still in a hardening phase before public launch. Expect rough edges.
💸
No market value today
MBITE is not listed on any exchange. It cannot be bought or sold for money today. Any coins you mine are real chain coins, but they currently carry zero monetary value. Do not make financial decisions based on expectations of a future price.
🧪
Experimental software
All cryptocurrency software — including MoonBite's — carries technical risk. Bugs, protocol changes, and unexpected behaviour are possible. The codebase is open source and auditable, but it is not infallible.
🔮
No guaranteed future value
Nothing about MoonBite's design, capped supply, or roadmap guarantees that MBITE will ever have monetary value. The exchange listing is on the roadmap, but a listing is not a price floor and not a promise of value.
Self-custody

You are the bank — entirely

Self-custody means the responsibility rests with you. These are not abstract risks.

🔑
Lose your keys, lose your coins
Your private key (or seed phrase) is the only proof of ownership of your MBITE. If you lose it — through device failure, accidental deletion, or physical loss of a written backup — there is no recovery option. Not from us, not from anyone. Write your seed phrase down on paper, store it somewhere safe, and back it up in at least two locations.
↩️
Transactions are irreversible
Once a transaction is confirmed on the chain, it cannot be reversed, recalled, or disputed. If you send MBITE to the wrong address, or if you're deceived into sending it, the coins are gone. Always double-check the full address before broadcasting a transaction — the first and last few characters are not enough.
🤫
Never share your seed phrase
No legitimate MoonBite service — including this website, the wallet, the miner, or the explorer — will ever ask for your seed phrase or private key. Any request for your seed phrase is a scam. Full stop.
🧾
Backups are your responsibility
MoonBite has no customer support team that can restore your wallet. Backing up your seed phrase is not a one-time task — it is an ongoing responsibility. If your backup is on a single piece of paper in one location, that is one fire, flood, or theft away from being gone.

Stay safe

How to protect yourself

The most common dangers are social engineering, fake software, and careless backups — not protocol bugs.

⬇️
Download only from official sources
The wallet, miner, and node software are distributed from this site and the official source repository. Don't install MoonBite software from third-party sites, social media links, or unsolicited messages.
🎭
Impersonators are common
Scammers routinely impersonate cryptocurrency projects, including their developers and social accounts. MoonBite will never DM you to offer help, give you "free MBITE," or ask you to send coins to a wallet.
✅
Verify addresses character by character
Some malware ("clipboard hijackers") replaces copied addresses with attacker-controlled ones. Before sending, verify the first and last several characters of the address match what you intended.
📡
Use your own node for high-value checks
A block explorer you don't control could theoretically show you false data. For any high-value transaction, confirm it via your own running node's RPC — the chain doesn't lie.
📵
Keep seed phrases offline
A seed phrase stored in a phone note, email draft, or cloud document is one data breach away from being stolen. Paper, metal stamp, or other offline media stored securely is the standard.
🔗
Know what a scam looks like
Common crypto scams include "double your MBITE" giveaways, fake support staff asking for seed phrases, and phishing sites mimicking moonbite.org. Read the scams guide before you interact with anyone claiming to be MoonBite.
