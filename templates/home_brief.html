So what does “solving a block” actually cost?

The work

What your computer is actually doing.

It is not “generating” coins, and it is not solving anything useful to anyone else. It is guessing, very fast, at a number that is hard to find and trivial to check.

01

It collects the transactions nobody has written down yet.

Payments waiting to be settled are gathered into a candidate block.

Detail

Mempool assembly. Your node validates each transaction against consensus rules — signatures, inputs, scripts — before it will include it. An invalid transaction costs you the block, so nodes check their own work first.

02

It guesses until a guess is small enough.

Billions of attempts, each one either under the threshold or discarded.

Detail

RandomX hashes the block header and compares it against the target implied by bits. Current difficulty —, retargeting every 60 blocks toward the 120-second interval. RandomX is memory-hard and tuned for general-purpose CPUs, so a desk machine stays viable.

03

When it wins, everyone else checks the work in a millisecond.

Expensive to produce, cheap to verify. That asymmetry is the whole system.

Detail

The solved header propagates and every node re-verifies it independently. The block subsidy of 10 MBITE is paid to the finder and becomes spendable after 100 further blocks — standard coinbase maturity, which protects against reorganisations.

Why would anyone design money that has to be expensive to make?

Why work

Money that is cheap to make is money somebody will make.

Every alternative eventually asks you to trust whoever holds the most of it, or whoever wrote the list of who may vote. Work is the only entry cost that cannot be granted to a friend. You cannot be added to it, and no one can be excluded from it.

MoonBite uses RandomX rather than SHA-256 deliberately. It is memory-hard and built for ordinary processors, so the advantage held by purpose-built hardware is small enough that a normal computer remains competitive. We will not claim more than that: resistance to specialised hardware is a position to be defended, not a property that is ever finally won.

And this costs electricity. That is the mechanism, not a footnote we would rather you skipped. A coin that costs nothing to create is worth precisely what it cost to create.

Objections

The questions worth asking.

Is this a scam?
The mechanism of nearly every crypto scam is selling you something. There is nothing here to buy — no sale, no allocation, no token round. Judge that absence first, then judge the code, which is linked above in full.
Why mine something with no price?
Because there is no way to acquire it later that does not depend on somebody choosing to sell. If it never has a price, you spent some electricity on a curiosity. That is the honest range of outcomes, and we are not going to narrow it for you.
Who is behind it?
The contributors are anonymous by choice. That is a cost as much as a principle: you get code you can audit instead of names you would have to trust. Any mining done by the people who built it is disclosed in ADR-006.
Isn't this just a copy of Litecoin?
In large part, yes — deliberately. Consensus code is the worst possible place to be original, and rewriting it from scratch would make this less safe, not more. Every parameter that differs is listed in the table above.
What happens if you disappear?
The network does not need this website. Node software, seed addresses and build instructions are public, and the chain continues for as long as anyone mines it. That is the test of whether a coin was ever really decentralised.
Will it be listed on an exchange?
Unknown, and not promised anywhere on this site. Listings are decided by exchanges, not by us, and any page that guarantees one is telling you something it cannot know.
