How It Works

Under the hood.

MoonBite isn't magic — it's a set of open rules that every participant enforces independently. Here's what's actually happening when you mine, send, or verify a transaction.

Proof-of-work

Mining: turning electricity into trust

Proof-of-work is the mechanism that lets strangers agree on a shared history without trusting each other.

⛏️

What miners actually do

A miner repeatedly hashes a candidate block header, tweaking a nonce value each time, until the result falls below a network-set target. Finding a valid hash requires real computational effort — you cannot fake it or shortcut it. The first miner to find a valid solution broadcasts the block and collects the reward.

🧂

Why scrypt?

MoonBite uses the scrypt hashing algorithm, the same one that has secured Litecoin since 2011. Scrypt is deliberately memory-hard, which raises the resource cost for any attacker trying to rewrite history. It also means consumer hardware — CPUs, GPUs, and scrypt ASICs — can all participate.

🎯

Difficulty adjustment

The network automatically recalculates the target difficulty so that blocks arrive at roughly the right rate even as more miners join or leave. MoonBite targets one new block every 150 seconds — about 2.5 minutes.

🛡️

What makes it secure

To rewrite a past transaction, an attacker would need to redo all the proof-of-work from that point forward — faster than the honest network is building on top of it. As the total hash rate grows, that attack becomes proportionally more expensive.

The chain

Blocks & the blockchain

Each block is a permanent, ordered record linked to every block before it.

📦

A block is a batch

A block contains a set of transactions waiting in the mempool, a reference (hash) of the previous block, and proof that valid mining work was done. Miners choose which transactions to include, typically prioritising those with higher fees.

🔗

Chaining creates permanence

Each block contains the hash of the block before it. Change anything in block 1,000 and you break the hash that block 1,001 expects — then 1,002, and every block after. That chain of proof-of-work makes history tamper-evident without a central authority.

⏳

Confirmations & finality

Once a transaction lands in a block, each additional block mined on top is another "confirmation." More confirmations means more cumulative work an attacker would need to undo. For most purposes, 6 confirmations (~15 minutes) is considered practically final.

Wallets & keys

Controlling coins with cryptography

There are no accounts on the MoonBite network — only keys and addresses.

🔑

Private key

A private key is a large random number generated on your device. It is the only proof of ownership of the coins at your address. Anyone with your private key can spend your coins; without it, nobody can — not even MoonBite developers. Guard it accordingly.

📬

Address (public)

Your address is derived from your private key through one-way cryptography. You can share your address freely — it is just a destination. MoonBite mainnet addresses start with moon1 (bech32 format); testnet addresses start with tmoon1.

✍️

Signing a transaction

When you send MBITE, your wallet uses your private key to produce a cryptographic signature that proves you authorised the spend — without ever revealing the key itself. Nodes check this signature before accepting the transaction.

🌱

Seed phrase

A seed phrase is a human-readable backup of your private key — typically 12 or 24 words. Write it down offline and keep it safe. If you lose your seed and your device fails, your coins are gone permanently.

Nodes

Nodes & consensus

The rules of MoonBite are enforced by every node running the software — not by any single company or server.

🖧

What a node does

A full node downloads every block since genesis, validates each one against the protocol rules, and relays valid blocks and transactions to peers. It is the software that actually enforces what MoonBite is.

🗳️

Consensus without a leader

Nodes agree on the state of the chain by each following the same rules independently. There is no vote and no administrator. If a block breaks a rule, every node that sees it will reject it, regardless of who produced it.

🔍

Why run your own?

Trusting someone else's node means trusting them to run the rules correctly. Running your own node gives you a direct, unmediated view of the real chain. You accept no block on faith — you verify it.

Emission

The fixed supply schedule

Every MBITE that will ever exist is created by miners according to a schedule written into the protocol at launch.

🧮

Hard cap: 19,999,999.87 MBITE

The protocol will never produce more than this. The asymptotic cap is a result of halving: rewards halve every million blocks and approach zero but never reach it exactly.

🎁

Block subsidy starts at 10 MBITE

The genesis block carries a reward of 10 MBITE, paid entirely to the miner who found it. There was no premine: no coins were created before public mining began.

📉

Halving every 1,000,000 blocks

At the current 2.5-minute block target, one million blocks takes roughly 4.75 years. After each halving the block reward is cut in half, permanently reducing the rate of new issuance.

The flow

A transaction, start to finish

✍️
1. Sign & broadcast
You sign a transaction with your private key and broadcast it to the network. It enters the mempool — a shared waiting room of unconfirmed transactions.
⛏️
2. Miner includes it
A miner picks up your transaction from the mempool, includes it in a candidate block, and races to find a valid proof-of-work for that block.
📦
3. Block is found
The winning miner broadcasts the completed block. Every node validates it, and if it's valid, adds it to its copy of the chain. Your transaction is now confirmed.
🔗
4. More blocks follow
Each subsequent block adds another layer of proof-of-work on top of yours. After six confirmations (~15 minutes), rewriting your transaction becomes computationally impractical.
🔍
5. Verify independently
Look up your transaction in the block explorer or via your own node. The record is public and permanent — no account required to check it.
✅
6. Receiver holds the coins
The recipient's wallet now shows the balance. Only their private key can sign the next transaction spending those coins — nobody else has that power.
