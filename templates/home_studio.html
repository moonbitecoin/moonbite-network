Ledger

Start at block zero.

The first block paid nothing to anyone. Every block after it paid exactly ten, and only to the machine that solved it. That is the whole issuance story, and it is public.

Ten a block sounds arbitrary. What decides who gets it?

The work

Nobody decides. A number does.

Three things happen on your machine, in order, for as long as you leave it on.

01

It gathers what hasn't been written down.

Unsettled payments are collected into a candidate block.

For engineers

Mempool assembly. Each transaction is validated against consensus rules — scripts, inputs, signatures — before inclusion, because an invalid one forfeits the block.

02

It guesses until a guess is small enough.

Billions of attempts. Each is either under the threshold or thrown away.

For engineers

RandomX hashes the header against the target implied by bits. Difficulty —, retargeting every 60 blocks toward the 120-second interval. Memory-hard and CPU-tuned, so commodity hardware stays viable.

03

Everyone else checks it instantly.

Slow to find, immediate to confirm. That imbalance is the security.

For engineers

The header propagates and each node re-verifies independently. The ten-MBITE subsidy matures after 100 blocks before it can be spent, which is what makes a reorganisation survivable.

Supply

It halves until there is nothing left to halve.

Ten becomes five, five becomes two and a half. No vote raises it: a chain that paid more is a chain every node already refuses.

A coin that stops paying its own miners has a problem. Ours is written down, not hidden: see ADR-011.

Why work

Cost is the only door nobody can be let through.

Every other way of deciding who gets new money ends up trusting whoever already holds the most of it, or whoever wrote the list of who may vote. Work cannot be granted to a friend.

RandomX is memory-hard and built for ordinary processors, which keeps the advantage of purpose-built hardware small. We will not say more than that: resistance to specialised hardware is a position defended over time, never a property finally won.

And it burns electricity. That is the mechanism, stated in the affirmative — a coin that costs nothing to make is worth what it cost to make.

Then the code had better be readable. It is, and most of it isn't ours.

Code

Twelve rows are the whole difference.

MoonBite Core is a fork of Litecoin Core, itself a fork of Bitcoin Core. We say that first so you don't have to find it. The inherited code carries a decade of review; what deserves attack is the delta below.

Objections

Ask the harder questions.

Is this a scam?
Scams sell. There is nothing here to buy — no round, no allocation, no sale. Weigh that absence, then weigh the code.
Why mine something worth nothing?
Because later there is no route in that doesn't depend on somebody agreeing to sell. If it never has a price, you spent some electricity on a curiosity. That is the full range of outcomes and we won't narrow it.
Who is behind this?
Anonymous contributors, by choice. That costs us your trust in names and buys you code you can read. Any founder mining is disclosed in ADR-006.
Isn't it just Litecoin renamed?
Largely, and on purpose — consensus code is the worst place to be inventive. The table above is every parameter that differs.
What if you vanish?
The chain does not need this site. Source, seeds and build steps are public; it runs as long as anyone mines it.
Will it get listed?
Unknown, and promised nowhere. Exchanges decide that, not us.
