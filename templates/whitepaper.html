Protocol Paper

The MoonBite design.

MoonBite does not claim to have invented a new consensus mechanism. It stands on two proven foundations — Bitcoin's double-spend solution and Litecoin's scrypt proof-of-work — and defines its own parameters on top of them. This document describes those design choices in original terms.

Foundations

What MoonBite inherits

Good engineering builds on what works. MoonBite does not reinvent two solved problems.

₿
From Bitcoin: the double-spend solution
Satoshi Nakamoto's 2008 paper solved the double-spend problem for digital cash without a trusted third party. The key insight: a chain of proof-of-work blocks, where the longest valid chain is the canonical history. An attacker who wants to rewrite the past must outpace the honest network in computational work — a cost that grows with the network's total hash rate. MoonBite inherits this model entirely. The UTXO transaction model, the block structure, and the security argument all trace directly to Bitcoin's design.
Ł
From Litecoin: scrypt proof-of-work
Litecoin (2011) introduced scrypt as an alternative to Bitcoin's SHA-256 for proof-of-work. Scrypt is memory-hard: completing a hash requires holding a large array in RAM throughout the computation. This increases the cost of building dedicated attack hardware and keeps the algorithm accessible to a broader range of consumer hardware. MoonBite uses scrypt for exactly these reasons — it is a battle-tested algorithm with over a decade of real-world security at scale.
Parameters

MoonBite's own design choices

These are the specific decisions that make MoonBite distinct from its parent chains.

⏱️

2.5-minute block target

A new block is targeted every 150 seconds. This is faster than Bitcoin's 10-minute target, reducing average wait time for a first confirmation. The network adjusts difficulty automatically to hold this target as hash rate changes.

🎁

10 MBITE genesis reward

The block subsidy at launch is 10 MBITE per block. This is paid entirely to the miner who solves the block. There is no founder reward, no pre-allocated treasury, and no developer cut. The emission is pure miner pay.

📉

Halving every 1,000,000 blocks

After each million blocks (~4.75 years at the 2.5-minute target), the block subsidy is cut in half. This mirrors Bitcoin's halving mechanism but on a block-count schedule matched to MoonBite's faster block time.

🧮

Cap: 19,999,999.87 MBITE

The total supply is asymptotically bounded. Each halving halves the rate of new issuance; the sum of all halvings converges to a finite limit just under 20 million MBITE. No additional coins can be created outside this schedule.

⚖️

No premine — fair launch

No MBITE was created before public mining began. The founders hold no privileged allocation. Every coin in existence was earned through open, competitive proof-of-work mining. Any early mining by the development team was publicly disclosed under ADR-006.

📬

Bech32 address format

MoonBite uses bech32 encoded addresses for all standard outputs. Mainnet addresses begin with moon1; testnet addresses begin with tmoon1. The format includes a built-in checksum that catches most copy-paste errors before a transaction is broadcast.

Design rationale

Why these choices were made

🤝

Why build on Litecoin's codebase?

Litecoin is one of the oldest continuously operating blockchain codebases in the world. Its consensus rules are battle-tested, its security model is well-understood, and its scrypt PoW algorithm has faced sustained adversarial pressure for over a decade. Building on a proven base reduces the attack surface from novel implementation bugs. MoonBite adds its own parameters and address format on top, rather than rewriting everything from scratch.

🛡️

Why scrypt over SHA-256?

SHA-256 mining is now almost entirely dominated by ASICs designed and manufactured by a small number of companies. Scrypt's memory hardness makes ASIC advantage less extreme and keeps CPU and GPU participation viable longer, supporting a more distributed initial mining ecosystem before ASIC miners arrive at scale.

📉

Why a lower initial supply cap?

MoonBite's maximum supply of roughly 20 million — rather than Bitcoin's 21 million — results from the 10-MBITE genesis reward and the 1,000,000-block halving schedule. This was not an arbitrary choice but a consequence of designing the emission curve around a specific block time and halving period. The math is transparent and verifiable in the source code.

🌱

Why no premine?

A premine — where founders create coins before public launch — is the single most common source of hidden conflicts of interest in cryptocurrency projects. Eliminating it entirely means every MBITE in existence was earned through publicly verifiable computational work. It also means the founders cannot secretly dump a large allocation on a future market. The fair-launch design is documented in ADR-006.
